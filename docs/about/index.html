About

Storm Wheeler
Project Management · Technical Operations · AI-assisted workflows

I operate at the intersection of technical systems, execution discipline, and long-term thinking.

My background spans manufacturing, electrical systems, and large-scale international live events. I began in hands-on technical environments—working directly with machinery, electrical infrastructure, and process optimization—which built a practical understanding of how systems fail, how they recover, and where friction actually comes from.

After relocating to the UAE, my focus shifted toward project management and site operations. The work moved from execution to coordination: aligning people, power, logistics, timelines, and safety in environments where delays and mistakes are highly visible and costly. This transition reinforced a core principle that still guides my work today—clarity and structure outperform urgency and noise.

How I approach problems

I do not optimize for speed alone. I optimize for repeatable outcomes.

That means:

  • Identifying failure points before they surface
  • Designing workflows that hold under pressure
  • Using documentation and structure to reduce decision load
  • Improving systems so the same issue is less likely to occur again

This approach applies equally to physical infrastructure, operational processes, and digital workflows.

Current direction

Alongside ongoing project and operational work, I am actively deepening my understanding of AI-assisted workflows and automation. The focus is not experimentation for its own sake, but selective application—where automation improves reliability, decision quality, or operational leverage without introducing hidden risk.

I am particularly interested in:

  • Automation that supports execution rather than replaces judgment
  • Systems that remain auditable and understandable under stress
  • Incremental improvements that compound over time

Long-horizon thinking

Beyond immediate delivery, I spend time studying systems that operate over long timeframes—commodities, monetary structures, and incentive-driven digital markets. These are viewed not as short-term opportunities, but as frameworks for understanding risk, durability, and structural alignment.

Discipline, order, and direction matter. The goal is not constant motion, but steady progress toward systems that are resilient, efficient, and intentionally designed.
