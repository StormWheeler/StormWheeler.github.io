<summary>
<strong>Project A — Electrical systems foundations (manufacturing environment)</strong><br>
<span style="opacity:0.75;">
Early-career delivery of full electrical infrastructure, spanning power distribution, control systems, and machine integration.
</span>
</summary>

<br>

Hands-on construction of a full electrical infrastructure for a production facility, covering power distribution, control systems, and machine integration.

The work involved installing and terminating the main incoming supply, assembling and wiring main and sub-distribution boards, and integrating multiple machine-level power and control circuits across the plant. Systems included power distribution, control panels, temperature regulation circuits, and dedicated control boards, all executed with attention to load management, safety, and long-term reliability.

Delivery was guided by electrical technical drawings and schematics, requiring accurate interpretation and on-site execution. This project marked my first sustained exposure to electrical design documentation and early-stage CAD-based layouts, where understanding how drawings translated into physical systems became critical to maintaining consistency and preventing downstream faults.

Although completed as part of a wider team, I took direct responsibility for significant portions of the installation and coordination, ensuring clean separation between power and control circuits and coherent integration from incoming supply through to individual machine endpoints.

This project formed the technical foundation for how I approach systems today: understanding how components interact, how design decisions manifest on-site, and why disciplined structure early in the build phase reduces operational issues later.

</details>

<summary>
<strong>Project B — Supervision and automation (production environment)</strong><br>
<span style="opacity:0.75;">
Mid-career transition into supervision, automation, and process improvement within a live production environment.
</span>
</summary>

<br>

Supervision and delivery of an automation-focused improvement project aimed at reducing manual labour and increasing throughput within an existing production line.

At this stage, my role expanded from technical execution into team supervision and on-site coordination. I was responsible for guiding a small team, maintaining installation quality, and ensuring work aligned with operational requirements while the facility remained active.

The project centred on analysing a labour-intensive workflow and designing a mechanical transfer solution to improve product flow between stages. This included the design and implementation of a conveyor-based system (V-belt configuration) that reduced repetitive manual handling, increased consistency, and allowed personnel to be redeployed to higher-value tasks elsewhere in the operation.

The work required close engagement with technical drawings and CAD-based layouts, bridging design intent with practical execution. In addition to supervising installation, I coordinated between production staff, technical teams, and management to ensure the system integrated cleanly into existing processes.

This phase marked a clear shift in responsibility: moving from building systems to overseeing their implementation, managing people, and improving how work flowed through the facility.

<details>
<summary>
<strong>Project C — Project management and large-scale temporary infrastructure delivery (live event environment)</strong><br>
<span style="opacity:0.75;">
Project-level ownership of complex, safety-critical temporary infrastructure supporting a large-scale live event.
</span>
</summary>

<br>

Project management and delivery responsibility for a large-scale temporary infrastructure deployment supporting an event population of approximately 5,000 people.

The scope covered the planning, build, and operational handover of a complex, time-bound site comprising over 50 cabins, more than 75 tents, multiple generator compounds, and an extensive temporary power and distribution network. Infrastructure included dozens of generators arranged across several compounds, over 100 distribution panels, and multiple kilometres of temporary cabling installed, tested, and commissioned within a constrained build window.

My responsibility sat at the project delivery layer: translating requirements into build plans, sequencing works, coordinating teams, and ensuring systems were installed safely, consistently, and to operational standards. Execution took place over an approximately three-month build period, with rotating teams typically ranging between five and ten personnel, operating under extended hours and evolving site conditions.

All systems were temporary by design, requiring disciplined installation practices, clear labelling, structured distribution layouts, and effective fault isolation. Power and site services were delivered with safety, uptime, and operational continuity as primary constraints, in alignment with the principles and requirements of BS 7909 for temporary electrical installations.

While delivered as part of a broader multi-disciplinary operation, I held responsibility for my scope from early planning through live operation and eventual handover. The project was delivered across consecutive years, with systems refined and processes stabilised to the point where on-site teams could operate and maintain the infrastructure independently.

This phase marked the transition from supervising work to managing delivery: balancing risk, coordinating people and systems at scale, and designing operational structures that remain reliable under real-world pressure.

</details>
