# Advice: Tessera feature claims vs product scope, 25 Nov 2026

**Asked by:** Website session (via the Tessera Marketing note that the product record covers room keys only). No change has been made; the owner decides.

## The claims angle
- **Misleading-claims risk is real even pre-sale.** The UAE Consumer Protection Law (Federal Law 15/2020) and the Media Council advertising standards treat describing features a product doesn't have as misleading. "Not for sale yet" lowers the risk but doesn't remove it. The home page invites hotels to "Request access", so it is promoting the product to businesses.
- **Where the claim appears.** If Tessera is keys-only, room climate, lighting and curtains are claimed in:
  - the Labs bullet "A room that is cool, lit and ready on arrival" (EN + 5 languages)
  - the home Tessera panel (31→22°C, Lights, Curtains, "Ready")
  - the home and Tessera film posters and frames (the room waking)
  - the Ads Day 6 Tessera slide "The room is ready before you reach the door"
  - the private /tessera page (step 03 "The room wakes up"), which is shown to hotels in meetings. A specific feature claim made directly to a business carries more risk than a mood line, not less.
- **The two sources disagree.** Decision 15 (16 Nov, owner) says "the room wakes at check-in approval", while the Tessera product record is said to say keys only. The owner decides the scope; the website then follows it.

## Recommendation
1. **If the scope is keys only (or room control isn't firmly on the roadmap):**
   - Labs bullet → "Your key is on your phone and your room is ready when you arrive." (Avoid "unlocked when you arrive": it suggests an unattended door opening, which is a safety and security claim.)
   - Home panel → relabel to arrival steps ("Check-in approved · Key ready · Room 1204"). Drop the temperature, lights and curtains.
   - The headline "The room is ready before you reach the door." can stay; "ready" is general.
   - Also update the Ads Day 6 Tessera slide and the private /tessera page step 03 before any further hotel meetings.
2. **If room control is genuinely planned:** keep the scene, but label it plainly in the panel or caption: "Concept: what Tessera is being designed to do. Features will vary by hotel." A label at the bottom of the page isn't enough when the panel shows specific settings.
3. Whichever applies, keep the translations in step with the English.

Not recommended: keeping specific climate or lighting settings with only a general "illustrative" note if the product will never control them. That is still a feature claim.
